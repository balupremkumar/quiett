# Naming — voice dictation product

Session date 2026-07-11.
Current working name "VoiceDictate" kept as the fallback if nothing here clears both the collision check and Balu's ear.

## Market note before the list

The offline, hold-to-talk, whisper.cpp-powered dictation niche got crowded fast through 2025 and into 2026.
Checking eight strong candidates below turned up direct, same-category competitors for six of them, several with almost the exact pitch this product has (hotkey, local Whisper, paste into any app, privacy as the headline).
Murmur alone has at least six separate apps using that name in this category.
The practical result: descriptive names built from "speak/talk/voice/dictate" and the obvious evocative metaphors are mostly taken.
A coined word or an unusual real-word pairing has a much better shot at being ownable.

## 1. Candidates (18, four lanes)

### Descriptive — says what it does

1. **LocalDictate** — plain compound, states the two facts that matter: it's dictation, and it stays local.
2. **OfflineVoice** — leads with the one claim that's actually provable, not just a vibe.
3. **PrivateType** — two ordinary words that name the privacy story and the output at once.
4. **SpeakLocal** — reads like an instruction, tells the user what happens and where the audio stays.

### Evocative — speed, flow, voice metaphors

5. **Flowspeak** — speech turning into flowing text, matches the hold-and-release rhythm of the hotkey.
6. **Wordwave** — sound waves becoming words, echoes the waveform already in the popup.
7. **Talkstream** — continuous talk becoming a running stream of text.
8. **Swiftspeak** — leans on speed, the daily win over typing by hand.
9. **Voicecraft** — implies control and precision, ties to the edit-before-paste step.

### Coined / invented

10. **Dictaro** — "dictate" with a soft invented ending, easy to say, sounds like a product not a feature.
11. **Vocora** — vocal plus an invented suffix, reads premium, no dictionary meaning to collide with.
12. **Quietype** — "quiet" and "type" fused into one word, carries the offline hush and the output in a single beat.
13. **Talkript** — "talk" and "script" blended, parses on first hearing despite being invented.
14. **Dictify** — dictate turned into an app-style verb, the same naming pattern as a lot of familiar SaaS.

### Short real words, repurposed

15. **Utter** — real word for speaking, doubles as "complete," would be excellent if it were free.
16. **Murmur** — a quiet spoken sound, matches the private, offline feel.
17. **Verbatim** — literally means word for word, about as literal a fit as a name gets.
18. **Diction** — an existing word for manner of speech, one syllable shorter than "dictation."

## 2. Collision check on the strongest 8

Checked by search for an existing product, app, or company with the same or a confusable name in dictation, transcription, or AI voice tooling. Domain notes only where a search actually surfaced a live site or a for-sale listing; otherwise marked unknown.

- **Utter** — taken, directly. Utter.to and utterai.app are both live AI dictation apps, one on Mac/iPhone, one as an Android voice keyboard, same "type by talking" pitch as this product. Kill.
- **Murmur** — taken, badly. At least six separate apps share the name in this exact category: a Windows offline Whisper app, a Mac voice-to-text app, "Murmur — Voice Dictation for Programmers," plus open-source variants on GitHub. Kill.
- **Dictaro** — taken, and it's close to a twin. dictaro.ai is a Windows dictation tool with a system-tray hotkey, waveform feedback, and cursor injection into VS Code, Word, Gmail. Same product shape as this one. Kill.
- **Verbatim** — taken as a product name too (an open-source cross-platform "press hotkey, talk, text lands locally" app), on top of the well-known Verbatim storage-media brand. Kill.
- **Voicecraft** — taken by a genuinely well-known research project and several commercial spinoffs (zero-shot voice cloning / speech editing, widely cited). Heavy collision in the AI-voice space specifically. Kill.
- **Wordwave** — taken. wordwave.app is a live transcription tool that markets itself as "privacy-first," plus a scattering of unrelated word-game apps. Kill.
- **PrivateType** — clear. No app, company, or product found under this name; only a .NET testing-framework class of the same name, irrelevant category. privatetype.com is listed for sale on BrandBucket, meaning it's not a live competing site and is buyable now.
- **Diction** — mostly clear. No dictation app trades under this exact name; the one live use is "Smith & Diction," a design and copywriting studio, unrelated category and low confusion risk. diction.com ownership not confirmed either way, marked unknown.

Two more worth a note since they came up naturally during the checks above:

- **Quietype** — clear as a software product (nothing found in dictation, transcription, or AI tooling). The name is already used by an ambient/electronic musician, quietype.com hosts their artist page. Different category, low confusion risk, but the domain is occupied and not flagged for sale, so it would likely need a workaround domain.
- **Flowspeak / Talkstream / SpeakLocal / Speakly / Vocable** — all taken by live, unrelated-but-nameable products (an English-speaking practice app, an internet talk-radio platform, a dialect-translator app, a well-known language-learning app with a million users, and an AAC communication app). None is a direct dictation competitor, but all reduce how ownable the name would be in search and app-store listings.

## 3. Shortlist, ranked

**1. PrivateType**
Pitch: the one thing this product can prove that every cloud dictation tool can't, said in two plain words.
Collision risk: low. Nothing in dictation or AI voice tooling found under this name; only an irrelevant .NET testing class.
Tagline: "Type by talking. Nothing leaves your machine."

**2. Quietype**
Pitch: a single invented word that carries both the offline hush and the typed result, more brand than description.
Collision risk: low for software; the name is already held by a small independent musician, which affects domain access more than market confusion.
Tagline: "Say it. It stays quiet. It comes out as text."

**3. Diction**
Pitch: the shortest possible cousin of "dictation," a real word people already half-know.
Collision risk: low-moderate. Clear of dictation-app competitors, but it's a common English word, which makes it harder to own in search and easy to genericise.
Tagline: "Your diction, typed, and it never left the room."

**4. Talkript**
Pitch: an invented blend of talk and script, unusual enough to be memorable without needing explanation.
Collision risk: low; nothing found under this exact name. Slightly more of a leap to spell correctly on first hearing than the top three.
Tagline: "Talk it. Talkript writes it down."

**5. OfflineVoice**
Pitch: the plainest possible descriptive name, no invention, no metaphor, states the differentiator outright.
Collision risk: low-moderate. No exact brand match, but "offline voice [x]" is now a crowded phrase across several competing apps (OpenWhispr, Voicetypr, VoicePad AI, Vocalinux all use the same language), so it reads generic rather than ownable.
Tagline: "Offline Voice. What you say stays on your PC."

## 4. Recommendation

**PrivateType.**

It's the only name in the shortlist that scored clean on the collision check, has a domain confirmed buyable right now (not just unclaimed, actually for sale, no chase-down needed), and states the real, provable claim instead of a mood. That claim, nothing ever leaves the machine, is the actual product story per the brief, not a marketing line layered on top. A name that says it outright saves the tagline and the FAQ some work, and it reads fine out loud, which matters for a product people will say the name of while recommending it to a colleague.

Quietype is the strongest runner-up if Balu wants more brand personality and less literal description, at the cost of a domain that needs sorting rather than a straight purchase.
